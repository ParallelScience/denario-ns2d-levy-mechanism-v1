**Title**: Distinguishing Memory-Driven vs. Increment-Driven Superdiffusion in 2D Turbulence via Surrogate Lagrangian Analysis

**Description**: This study will identify the physical origin of the observed $H \approx 0.88$ superdiffusion by testing whether the heavy tails in tracer displacement PDFs arise from long-range temporal correlations or heavy-tailed velocity distributions. We will compute the Lagrangian velocity autocorrelation function $R_v(\tau)$ and the displacement PDF $P(\Delta x, \tau)$ using the 8000-tracer dataset (dt=0.4). To isolate the mechanism, we will perform a surrogate data test by phase-randomizing the Fourier transform of the velocity series to destroy temporal memory while preserving the single-time velocity PDF. Comparing the diffusion exponent $H$ of the original trajectories against these memory-less surrogates will reveal if the superdiffusion is a memory-driven process (CTRW-like) or an increment-driven Lévy flight. Finally, we will calculate the distribution of displacement "jumps" at varying lags $\tau$ to determine if the heavy tails are robust features of the velocity increments $\delta v(\tau)$. This Lagrangian-centric approach bypasses the temporal limitations of the vorticity snapshots, providing a definitive classification of the underlying stochastic process.
