**Title: Distinguishing Spectral Cascade versus Spatial Intermittency as Drivers of Superdiffusion**

This study determines if the observed $H \approx 0.88$ superdiffusion arises from the large-scale correlated flows of the inverse energy cascade or from intermittent strain-field regions. We will first compute the Eulerian energy spectrum $E(k)$ from the 15 vorticity snapshots to identify the spectral index within the $k \in [3, 6]$ forcing range and the inverse cascade regime, using the relation between Eulerian roughness $\xi$ and the Lagrangian velocity autocorrelation $R_v(\tau)$ to predict the theoretical stable index $\alpha$. To test for spatial intermittency, we will compute the cross-correlation between the local Eulerian vorticity $\omega(x, y)$—sampled at the 15 available time points—and the instantaneous Lagrangian acceleration $a(t) = dv/dt$ of tracers currently occupying those coordinates. By comparing the Lagrangian velocity autocorrelation $R_v(\tau)$—calculated at high frequency ($dt=0.4$)—against the spectral energy distribution, we will isolate whether the heavy tails in displacement PDFs are a consequence of the long-memory dynamics inherent to the inverse cascade ($k^{-3}$ spectrum) or impulsive jumps induced by localized high-strain regions. This approach replaces invalid temporal trapping metrics with a rigorous spectral-Lagrangian mapping, providing a definitive physical basis for the anomalous diffusion observed in the tracer ensemble.
